What it fixes

  • Level — gain or limiting into the −23…−18 dB RMS window with peaks held under −3 dB
  • Channels — the odd stereo chapter in a mono book (or vice versa) unified to the book's layout
  • Room tone — missing head tone added, over-long tails trimmed, using the chapter's own room tone carved from its longest quiet stretch, not a synthetic substitute
  • Format — 128k VBR, 48 kHz, WAV-from-the-studio, whatever you hand it: one final encode to 192 kbps CBR MP3 at 44.1 kHz
  • One book, one run — a whole folder of chapters per pass, each re-checked; originals never modified

What it cannot fix — honestly

  • Pauses over 5 seconds are flagged, never auto-cut. A long pause might be a mistake — or the most dramatic beat in the chapter. AudiobookSpecFix lists them with timestamps; the decision stays yours.
  • A noise floor recorded too hot can't be level-fixed. If the hiss sits at −50 dB, pushing levels around just moves the problem between the RMS window and the floor limit. The report marks the chapter UNFIXABLE and tells you to re-record — no fake "fixed" file, no silent noise-reduction artefacts in your narration.
  • Passing the machine spec is not passing ACX. These are the machine-checkable technical requirements. ACX also runs human QC — pronunciation, performance, mouth clicks, extraneous sounds. AudiobookSpecFix can't hear those, and it won't pretend to.

Verified, not vibes

In a seeded acceptance test, 6 synthesised chapters carried planted defects: RMS at −30.3 dB, a −0.8 dBTP peak spike, a 48 kHz VBR encode with a 6.0 s interior pause, a chapter with 0.00 s of head tone and an 8.00 s tail, a stereo chapter in a mono book, and a −49.8 dB noise floor designed to be unfixable. 102 of 102 checks passed: chapters 1–5 were fixed and re-measured green on every check (RMS to −20.8 / −22.3 dB, peak to −3.9 dBTP, head tone 0.00 → 0.75 s, tail 8.00 → 2.50 s, the VBR chapter re-encoded with its 6 s pause flagged and kept at 25.2 → 25.2 s), chapter 6 was reported UNFIXABLE with no output file, zero new defects were introduced, and the originals' SHA-256 hashes were unchanged.
